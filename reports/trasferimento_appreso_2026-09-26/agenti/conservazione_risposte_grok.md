## 1. Conserved versus context-specific target responses

**Replogle et al., Cell 2022** (K562 dCas9-KRAB vs RPE1 dCas9-ZIM3; essential-gene overlap). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9380471/

- **Measured.** Same-line phenotypes are much more alike than cross-line phenotypes. K562 day 8 vs K562 day 6: cophenetic correlation of perturbation relationships 0.82, median Pearson of expression profiles r = 0.50. K562 day 8 vs RPE1 day 7: cophenetic correlation 0.37, median r = 0.23 (union of 1,206 perturbations with >10 DEGs in all three datasets; z-scored profiles on genes >0.25 UMI/cell).
- **Measured.** Growth phenotypes still correlate: r = 0.53, n = 1,951 constructs. DEG counts: Spearman ρ = 0.53, n = 2,636 shared perturbations. Chromosomal-instability scores: r = 0.69 across K562 and RPE1.
- **Measured.** They ran KEGG 2021 enrichment for perturbations with well-correlated profiles (r > 0.5) versus a second set the caption calls “uncorrelated (r > 0)”. Pathway names are only in the figure; the text does not list them. Within K562, the genotype–phenotype map recovers proteasome-subunit induction after proteasome loss, cholesterol-biosynthesis induction after trafficking defects, and a split between the unfolded-protein response (ER chaperones and translocation) and the integrated stress response (mitochondrial proteins, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, translation initiation). Those are within-K562 observations, not cross-line correlations.
- **Measured.** Nuclear responses to mitochondrial-gene knockdowns are relatively homogeneous and ISR-dominated (K562). The 13 mitochondrially encoded transcripts separate respiratory complexes and mitoribosome subunits (random-forest accuracy 0.64 on the mitochondrial transcriptome vs 0.25 on the nuclear transcriptome).
- **Measured.** p53 status changes the consequence of aneuploidy, not a genome-wide transfer correlation: abnormal-karyotype RPE1 cells (p53-positive) sit in G1/G0 at fraction 0.68 versus 0.44 for stable karyotype; K562 (p53-deficient) shifts less. Severe gain-plus-loss karyotypes upregulate the ISR in RPE1.

**Nadig et al., Nat Genet 2025** (same essential-gene CRISPRi set in K562, RPE1, Jurkat, HepG2; TRADE). Preprint text with the numbers: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244993/ ; published paper: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02169-3

- **Measured.** Raw log-fold-change correlation between K562 replicate screens has median 0.16; TRADE transcriptome-wide-impact correlation has median 0.90. Across cell types, mean correlation of transcriptome-wide impact is 0.62.
- **Measured.** On 1,660 perturbations with non-null impact in all four lines, median effect correlation is 0.74 (K562–Jurkat), 0.75 (HepG2–RPE1), 0.69 (Jurkat–HepG2), 0.64 (K562–HepG2), 0.56 (RPE1–Jurkat), 0.40 (K562–RPE1). A two-component mixture: 56% of perturbations stay high everywhere (mean 0.75 within the similar pairs, 0.66 outside); 44% drop outside similar pairs (0.61 within, 0.35 outside). Examples named in the text: DDX41 is broadly shared; NIFK is pair-specific.
- **Inferred by the authors, not a controlled test.** They note that the high pairs match p53 status and growth mode (K562/Jurkat: hematopoietic, p53-mutant, suspension; HepG2/RPE1: epithelial, p53-wild-type, adherent).
- **Measured, cell-type outliers of magnitude.** 241 perturbations sit >1.64 SD off the cross-line fit (K562 47, RPE1 118, Jurkat 10, HepG2 66), including GATA1 in K562 and HMGCR in Jurkat. A typical genome-wide knockdown affects an estimated 45 genes; a typical essential-gene knockdown affects more than 500.

**Jiang et al., Nat Cell Biol 2025** (Mixscale; not genome-wide). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12083445/

- **Measured.** 1,596 regulator knockdowns across six lines (A549, MCF7, HT29, HAP1, BxPC3, K562), all dCas9-KRAB-MeCP2, five stimuli. IFNγ and IFNβ downstream genes are described as extensively conserved across all six lines; the first IFNγ programme is “tightly conserved”. TGFβ and insulin are cell-type specific (TSC1/TSC2 broad; IRS1/GRB2 restricted). MultiCCA returned no clear insulin programme “due to extensive heterogeneity”. They report 31 cross-line programmes and 133 additional within-line signatures. No Pearson correlation of whole signatures across lines is given.
- **Measured, transfer of the signature, not of each target.** Conserved IFNβ genes (73 shared with MSigDB Hallmark, 227 unique to Perturb-seq) separate stimulated from resting monocytes. Cell-line-specific programmes learned from HT29 or other lines did not enrich a spatial colitis dataset, whereas the cross-line TGFβ programme did. That comparison is qualitative (Supplementary Fig. 15); no correlation is stated.

**Zhu, Dann, Marson and colleagues, bioRxiv 2025 / Cell 2026** (primary CD4+ T cells vs K562). https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696273v1.full

- **Measured.** About 8% of trans-effects detected in CD4+ T cells, across 3,081 shared perturbations, were also significant in K562. For 1,880 perturbations with a measurable K562 effect, mean Pearson correlation of the differential-expression profile is 0.32, above random pairs and below donor replicates. The highest-correlation targets are enriched for transcription, chromatin remodelling, telomere maintenance and cell-cycle regulation. The Cell paper summarises the same comparison, plus Jurkat, as “only partial sharing” (Figure S11, Note S4): https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00929-3
- **Measured, within one cell type.** About one third of 111 regulator clusters are coherent in only one or two of rest, 8 h and 48 h stimulation. Mediator and SAGA correlate as a complex in every condition but change which genes they move. Cytokine control is condition-specific (IL-2 and IL-13 negative regulators seen at rest; positive regulators after stimulation).

**X-Atlas/Orion (Huang et al., bioRxiv 2025)** does not report a HCT116-versus-HEK293T perturbation-profile correlation. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.11.659105v1.full.pdf

- **Measured, and easy to misread.** Median on-target knockdown is 75.4% in HCT116 and 51.5% in HEK293T (both dCas9-KRAB-Zim3). A single-cell classifier calls a phenotype for 48.36% of expressed-gene perturbations in HCT116, 40.37% in HEK293T, and 54.30% in the Replogle K562 set. The median Spearman of 0.74 is between non-targeting cells in randomly paired HCT116 and K562 batches, not a perturbation-transfer correlation.

**X-Atlas/Pisces and X-Cell (Xaira preprint, March 2026; not peer-reviewed).** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.18.712807v1 and https://www.cdn.xaira.com/papers/X_CELL_V1_0316_final.pdf

- **Measured.** Seven genome-scale CRISPRi screens (HCT116, HEK293T, HepG2, iPSC, resting Jurkat, stimulated Jurkat, differentiating iPSC). Fraction of perturbations with a significant phenotype ranges from 25.32% (HEK293T) to 72.80% (iPSC). Mean detectability (F1) correlates with mean feature overlap (Jaccard) at Spearman 0.642 (p < 0.001), but even the strong, relatively context-independent perturbations still have low absolute feature overlap. Perturbations with high effect in every context are enriched for respiratory electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation. Context-restricted perturbations enrich for cell-type identity pathways. The preprint does not name cholesterol, SREBP, Mediator or the proteasome as conserved programmes.

**Pan, Saunders, Replogle, Weissman and Zhuang, bioRxiv 2026** (re-analysis of K562 and RPE1 Perturb-seq, plus a new hESC screen). https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.16.725005v1.full

- **Measured.** 54 gene programmes (8–448 genes). An “essential” class includes chromatin, DNA replication/repair, cell cycle, splicing and ribosome biogenesis, expressed in proliferative cells. Ribosomal-protein genes, mitochondrially encoded genes, UPR, ISR, p53 and lysosome/autophagy vary more across perturbations than across cell types. Cholesterol biosynthesis is required everywhere but is highest in glia, immature neurons and liver. Interferon and TNF programmes are shared across immune, mesenchymal and epithelial cells at baseline.
- **Measured, from the paper’s results text as indexed.** p53 and lysosome/autophagy are activated by nearly all perturbation classes; UPR and ISR are selective. Mitochondrial transcription and translation activate the ISR in K562 but not in RPE1. Ribosomal-protein genes move opposite to ribosome-biogenesis genes, and large-subunit biogenesis knockdown decreases ribosomal-protein expression in K562 but increases it in RPE1. Cosine similarity of the same perturbation’s expression shift falls as the two starting cell states get farther apart on their manifold (Figure 4A); they do not publish a single correlation coefficient for that plot.
- **Inferred.** This is a learned decomposition of existing Perturb-seq, not a new paired measurement of every complex. It is the clearest source that separates ribosome biogenesis (conserved essential axis) from the ribosomal-protein readout (stress-like and sign-flipping).

Programmes the question named and that these papers do **not** quantify across cell types: Mediator (Replogle and Zhu treat it as a complex whose downstream genes change with context; no cross-line r), SREBP/cholesterol as a transferable knockdown signature (Replogle sees cholesterol induction inside K562 after trafficking defects; Pan sees basal cholesterol expression as cell-type biased), and the proteasome as a cross-line correlation (within-K562 only in Replogle).

## 2. Readout genes that move in many knockdowns

- **Measured, Nadig.** In K562 genome-wide Perturb-seq, genes with a strong growth effect are enriched 4.22× for the impact of perturbing them, but depleted to 0.71× as response genes. Highly expressed genes are enriched both as targets (2.26×) and as responders (4.44×). Loss-of-function-constrained genes are depleted as responders (0.40×). A “DE prior” gene set, genes often differential in published RNA-seq, is enriched as a response; stably expressed genes are depleted. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244993/
- **Measured, Pan et al. 2026.** p53 signalling and lysosome/autophagy behave as generic perturbation readouts; UPR and ISR do not. Ribosomal-protein genes are a stress readout and are regulated separately from ribosome-biogenesis factors, including a K562-versus-RPE1 sign flip. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.16.725005v1.full
- **Measured, Replogle.** The p53 difference is specific: aneuploidy drives G1/G0 arrest in RPE1 (0.68 vs 0.44) and much less in p53-deficient K562, and the strongest karyotypes induce the ISR in RPE1. Cell-cycle and total-RNA shifts (S phase and mitosis raise total RNA; general transcription factors, Mediator and elongation factors lower it) are under genetic control and reproducible across the K562 time points and RPE1, but no correlation coefficient is stated for those composite scores. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9380471/
- **Measured, Zhu.** After correcting for expression, immune genes (cytokines and receptors) have more incoming regulators than expected, and this is strongest at 8 h after stimulation. Median cross-guide correlation of significant effects is 0.49 (rest), 0.47 (8 h) and 0.52 (48 h). https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696273v1.full
- **Not found as a number.** No paper above reports the fraction of knockdowns that move a named p53 target such as CDKN1A, or a correlation of a p53-module score with TP53 genotype across a Perturb-seq panel.

## 3. CRISPRi cis effects

- **Measured, Replogle 2022.** About 7.5% of perturbations knock down a neighbouring gene (the immediate upstream or downstream gene expressed at >0.1 UMI/cell). Figure S3 plots knockdown against distance to the neighbour’s nearer end, separately in K562 and RPE1, and compares the two lines, but the text gives no distance cutoff, no percent knockdown by bin, and no divergent-versus-same-strand split. Worked bidirectional examples: ATF5 guide represses NUP62 (shared promoter, chromosome 19) and phenocopies nuclear-pore knockdown; TMEM215, CLOCK, and PGAM5/RMDN3 phenotypes are likewise explained by neighbour knockdown. Median on-target knockdown is 85.5% in K562 (dCas9-KRAB) and 91.6% in RPE1 (ZIM3). Those two numbers confound effector with cell line. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9380471/
- **Measured, Zhu 2025.** Downregulation of a nearby TSS is strongest within 5 kb (60% of 1,782 genes that have a proximal TSS; 18% of all tested perturbations) and weaker at 5–10 kb. They do not split by promoter orientation. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696273v1.full
- **Measured, Rosenbluh et al., Nat Commun 2017.** KRAB-dCas9 essentiality screens call extra essentials at bidirectional promoters. Genes up to 1 kb from a core essential are more often scored essential. At least 12.9% of protein-coding genes share a bidirectional promoter within 1 kb; the discussion says bidirectional promoters may affect up to 10% of human genes. This is a viability screen, not RNA measured by distance. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5457492/
- **Measured, Gilbert et al., Cell 2014.** For dCas9-KRAB, active guides concentrate from −50 to +300 bp of the TSS, with a maximum about 50–100 bp downstream. https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(14)01178-7
- **Measured, Yeo et al., Nat Methods 2018.** dCas9-KRAB-MeCP2 beat dCas9-KRAB for 15 of 25 guides tiled from −1 kb to +1 kb at CANX and SYVN1, on both strands, including outside the −50 to +200 bp window. Nearest neighbours of CXCR4 (about −274, −208, +651, +1,849 and +2,387 kb) were not significantly repressed. In HAP1, guides inside the optimal window were more often depleted than guides outside it, for both repressors; KRAB-MeCP2 was the stronger of the two inside and outside the window. No percent knockdown-versus-distance curve for endogenous neighbours is given. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-018-0048-5
- **Measured, same-cell-line effector comparison, Replogle et al., eLife 2022.** https://elifesciences.org/articles/81856 In K562, strong guides deplete surface proteins >96.8% (median) for Zim3-dCas9, dCas9-Kox1 and dCas9-Kox1-MeCP2. Weak guides: Kox1-MeCP2 strongest, Zim3 close, Kox1 weaker. Zim3 leaves about 5% of cells without detectable knockdown; Kox1-MeCP2 leaves 15–20%. Figure 4 annotations: Jurkat B2M knockdown 93.6% with Zim3-dCas9 versus 61.3% with dCas9-Kox1; RPE1 B2M 97.4% versus 93.4% on the same plot. Alerasool et al. (Nat Methods 2020) report ZIM3 KRAB stronger than KOX1 KRAB and than KOX1 KRAB–MeCP2 in HEK293T and K562 reporters, and better on 4 of 5 endogenous genes; the HTML does not state a fold change. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-020-0966-x
- **Not found.** No Perturb-seq paper retrieved here gives a numeric repression-versus-distance curve split by divergent versus same-strand promoters, or a cis-effect difference between dCas9-KRAB, KRAB-MeCP2 and Zim3 at matched loci.

## 4. Features a per-(target, response gene) model can use

Each is a prior on how much of the source effect should carry to a new cell type. None is a fitted weight.

1. **Target is a housekeeping complex versus a lineage regulator.** Evidence: Nadig medians 0.74–0.75 inside similar lines versus 0.40 for K562–RPE1, with 56% versus 44% of targets in the shared versus pair-specific class; Zhu mean r = 0.32 and only ~8% shared edges, enriched for transcription, chromatin, telomere and cell cycle; X-Cell high-everywhere targets enrich for respiratory electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation (Spearman 0.642 between detectability and Jaccard, but absolute overlap stays low). Compute: CORUM or Reactome membership of the target (mitoribosome, OXPHOS, spliceosome, proteasome, RNA polymerase) versus a lineage-TF list. GATA1 is the published negative control (Nadig K562 outlier).

2. **Interferon-pathway membership of the target.** Evidence: Jiang finds IFNγ and IFNβ target genes conserved across six lines and 31 cross-line programmes, while insulin yields none and TGFβ is context-specific; conserved programmes transferred to monocytes and to spatial colon, line-specific ones did not. This matches the team’s own observation that interferon targets transfer. Compute: target in the Jiang IFNβ/IFNγ regulator list or in Hallmark interferon / JAK-STAT, as a binary prior of high carry-over. Do not use insulin or TGFβ regulators the same way.

3. **Response gene is a generic stress or cell-cycle gene, interacted with TP53.** Evidence: Pan — p53 and lysosome/autophagy move after nearly all perturbation classes; Replogle — aneuploidy arrests RPE1 (G1/G0 0.68 vs 0.44) and not p53-null K562. Compute: response gene in Hallmark p53, a cell-cycle set, or a lysosome/autophagy set, times whether source and destination agree on TP53 wild-type versus mutant (DepMap or CCLE). Down-weight p53-target transfer into TP53-mutant lines.

4. **Response gene is a ribosomal protein versus the target is a ribosome-biogenesis factor.** Evidence: Pan — these are different programmes; biogenesis knockdown lowers ribosomal-protein expression in K562 and raises it in RPE1. Compute: two indicators, not one “ribosome” flag. A single ribosome score will average a conserved essential axis with a sign-flipping stress readout.

5. **ISR / UPR selectivity, with a mitochondrial exception.** Evidence: Replogle separates ISR from UPR inside K562 and finds mitochondrial knockdowns share one nuclear ISR response; Pan finds mitochondrial transcription and translation induce ISR in K562 but not RPE1. Compute: response gene in an ISR list (ATF4/DDIT3 and amino-acid-deprivation genes) or a UPR list, and a flag that the target is a nuclear-encoded mitochondrial gene. Transfer the ISR block from K562 into RPE1-like cells only with a penalty.

6. **Neighbour / divergent-promoter indicator on the pair.** Evidence: Replogle ~7.5% neighbour knockdown and the ATF5–NUP62 bidirectional case; Zhu 60% of genes with a TSS within 5 kb are downregulated (18% of perturbations); Rosenbluh 12.9% of protein-coding genes share a bidirectional promoter within 1 kb, and essentials bleed to 1 kb. Compute from GENCODE: distance from the guide’s target TSS to the response-gene TSS; orientation (head-to-head within 1 kb versus same strand); expression of that neighbour in the source. If the response gene is that neighbour, the source “trans” effect is cis and should not be transferred as biology. This is the feature most likely to be misread as conservation.

7. **On-target knockdown and dose, as a nuisance and as a biological modifier.** Evidence: Orion median knockdown 75.4% versus 51.5% in two Zim3 lines, and phenotype rates 48.36% versus 40.37%; Nadig, via Jost titration, finds weak versus strong depletion only moderately correlated (Sox9 r = 0.60, Polycomb r = 0.48), so dose changes which genes move. Compute: source knockdown fraction (or sgRNA UMI, which Orion correlates with knockdown at R = 0.91 and 0.90) and whether the destination expresses the target. Scale the transferred vector by knockdown, and allow a non-linear dose term rather than one profile per gene.

8. **Effector mismatch.** Evidence: eLife 2022 — weak guides and Jurkat B2M (93.6% Zim3 vs 61.3% Kox1); Yeo — KRAB-MeCP2 stronger than KRAB inside and outside the −50 to +200 bp window; the screens themselves use KOX1 (K562 Replogle), ZIM3 (RPE1, Orion, Jurkat Perturb-seq) or KRAB-MeCP2 (Jiang). Compute: a categorical source-effector feature. It predicts apparent effect size, not biological conservation. Without it, Zim3-versus-KOX1 differences will be learned as cell-type biology.

9. **Response-gene detectability.** Evidence: Nadig — highly expressed genes are 4.44× enriched as responders and constrained genes are at 0.40×; Zhu — incoming edges track baseline expression, and 70.8% (17/24) of MSigDB interferon genes missing from Jiang’s list were below 20 CPM. Compute: log mean expression of the response gene in the destination line, plus gnomAD loss-of-function constraint. Low expression predicts a zero in the destination even when the biology transferred.

10. **Basal transcriptome distance — include it so the model can learn that it fails.** Evidence for a relationship: Pan Figure 4A, shift-vector cosine depends on starting-state distance. Evidence against using it alone: the team’s Mixscale result that whole-transcriptome similarity does not predict transfer, and X-Cell’s result that even strong perturbations keep low gene-set overlap. Compute: cosine of control pseudobulks, but expect it to lose to features 1–3. Nadig’s similar-versus-distant pair structure (0.75 vs 0.35) is a better coarse prior than a continuous expression correlation.
